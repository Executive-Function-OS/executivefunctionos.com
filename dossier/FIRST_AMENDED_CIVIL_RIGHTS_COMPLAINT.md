## III. STATEMENT OF OPERATIVE FACTS

### A. The Water Shutoff

11. On May 31, 2024, Plaintiff Eriksson submitted a written Low-Income Reduced Rate Application to the City. In that application, she disclosed her documented disabilities, recent sepsis hospitalization, and that she was actively litigating quiet title in Clackamas County Circuit Court (Case No. 24CV21417), challenging municipal liens against 12054 Chapin Court.

12. On June 20, 2024, the City issued a "Shut Off Notice" stating that $418.49 was due and that service would be disconnected if payment was not made by June 20, 2024. The notice did not inform Plaintiffs of any right to dispute the charges, any procedure for protesting a disputed bill before an empowered municipal employee, any right to a pre-termination hearing, any payment plan options, or any medical emergency protections. Under Oregon City Municipal Code Chapter 13.04 (including OCMC §§ 13.04.040, 13.04.140, and 13.04.220), municipal water service is terminable only for specified cause.

13. On June 25, 2024, City utility staff physically disconnected municipal water service to 12054 Chapin Court over an account subject to dispute, without providing a pre-termination hearing or written notice of dispute procedures.

14. On June 27, 2024, Public Works utility crews acting under the operational direction of Defendant Vance Walker removed the water meter under Work Order 24-000915 and assessed a $120.00 meter-pull fee.

15. On July 8, 2024, Defendant Vance Walker formally authorized the physical deployment under Work Order 24-000915 of a multi-ton concrete vault block over the residential water connection (completed July 10, 2024). This physically barred water access and added an unauthorized $1,194.12 installation surcharge to Plaintiffs' account.

16. In early July 2024, during an extreme Pacific Northwest heatwave with temperatures reaching 95°F to 100°F, Plaintiff Buckhout suffered an acute medical crisis without running water due to heat-sensitive autoimmune Graves' disease. Following that crisis, Plaintiff Buckhout went in person to Oregon City Hall with cash to pay the delinquent balance. City Utility Billing personnel, acting under the customer accounting direction of Defendant Ashley Fraijo, refused to accept his cash tender and stated they could not determine the amount owed.

17. On July 24, 2024, Plaintiff Eriksson offered in writing to pay the $724.96 active delinquent utility balance. Defendant Ashley Fraijo of Utility Billing refused the payment in writing and demanded $1,919.08 in cash, conditioning service restoration on payment of concrete barrier fees and administrative surcharges.

18. The City's demands escalated from $143.89 to $418.49 to $724.96 to $1,919.08 to $4,289.55, while the underlying utility account remained subject to disputed fees.

### B. Code Enforcement and Retaliation

19. On July 22, 2024, Defendant Todd Kennedy hand-delivered a Notice of Violation citing 12054 Chapin Court for lacking running water. The notice was printed with a backdated date of June 22, 2024, three days prior to the actual shutoff. Defendant Kennedy stated that Plaintiffs faced immediate abatement and removal within five days.

20. On August 21, 2024, Plaintiff Eriksson submitted a formal written Request for Reasonable Accommodation under Title II of the ADA. The request was transmitted via email to municipal officials, including City Manager Tony Konkol. Plaintiff detailed her documented disabilities and requested three accommodations: (1) structured ADA administrative mediation; (2) a reasonable payment plan to restore water; and (3) an administrative review of code enforcement notices.

21. On August 22, 2024, the City responded in writing that Utility Billing would address Plaintiff's accommodation request. No City official engaged in an interactive dialogue. Subsequently, Defendant Tony Konkol telephoned Plaintiff Eriksson, declined to evaluate accommodations, and stated that pursuant to City administrative policy (Management Policy 3-29), the City would not modify its billing or shutoff procedures and would maintain the water disconnection until all disputed fees were paid.

22. On September 3, 2024, following a bench trial in Municipal Court, the court entered a General Judgment finding Plaintiff Eriksson not guilty of all Dangerous Building violations. Defendant Kennedy was present at the proceeding.

23. On September 4, 2024, less than 24 hours after the court acquittal, Defendant Kennedy arrived at Plaintiffs' residence accompanied by five police officers, ordered occupants off the property under threat of arrest, and posted a red "UNSAFE: DO NOT ENTER OR OCCUPY" placard backdated to August 4, 2024, without obtaining a judicial warrant.

24. On November 1, 2024, City Building Official James Sayers issued a formal dangerous building determination finding that without running water, sewer gases enter the living areas through dry plumbing traps, creating a dangerous health hazard. Despite affirmatively recognizing that the City's water shutoff caused this active sewer gas hazard, Defendants refused to restore water and used the condition to maintain red-placarding against the residence.

### C. Suppression of Curing Check and Retaliatory Notices

25. On November 5, 2024, a third-party escrow curing check for $2,863.77 was delivered to the City utility billing department referencing 12054 Chapin Court, matching the exact total of all three tracked utility accounts at that address. Defendant Ashley Fraijo did not credit the payment to the account, retained the check funds in City custody for four months, and disclosed confidential account details to third parties attempting to take over the property, before returning the check unapplied in March 2025 stating that the City could not determine what the payment was for.

26. On March 24, 2025, Plaintiffs served a formal Oregon Tort Claim Notice on the City. Less than three months later, on June 16, 2025, Defendant Kennedy signed and issued a Code Enforcement demand letter to Plaintiff Eriksson giving her 10 days to comply. On June 30, 2025, the City issued a formal Notice of Violation naming Defendant Kennedy as investigator, threatening $300.00 daily civil penalties while the home was subjected to the ongoing water shutoff.

### D. Ongoing Deprivation and ADA Denials

27. The water remained physically disconnected for 510 days, from June 25, 2024 through November 16, 2025, inclusive.

28. On November 14, 2025, Clackamas County Circuit Court issued a Temporary Restraining Order commanding the City to restore safe, potable water by November 16, 2025.

29. On Sunday, November 16, 2025, retained City Counsel Ramon Henderson arrived at Plaintiffs' residence accompanied by a City Public Works utility crew operating under Work Order 25-001106 authorized by Defendant Walker. The crew removed the concrete vault block, reconnected the water meter, and repaired the service connection. Defendant Henderson stated on site that the City would not permit the supply valve to be opened that day, asserting potential pipe bursts and the need for a system flush. When Plaintiff Buckhout responded that the interior plumbing was secured under a plumber's guidance, Defendant Henderson refused to accept a standard waiver on site, stated he needed to draft a customized waiver involving case law, and conditioned immediate compliance with the TRO on Plaintiffs executing an un-drafted release of liability. Following Defendant Henderson's departure, Plaintiff Buckhout opened the curb stop valve, restoring potable water and ending the 510-day physical shutoff. In February 2026, the City inflated the utility ledger to $4,289.55 and issued renewed shutoff notices demanding payment under threat of immediate disconnection, which the City paused only after Plaintiffs gave notice of intent to initiate federal civil rights litigation.

30. In May and June 2026, City code enforcement personnel issued citations targeting curtilage debris and unmaintained vegetation, conditions directly resulting from the City's prolonged 510-day water shutoff and the termination of City-franchised solid waste service during the utility dispute, which left disabled residents without standard disposal utilities. Concurrently, Plaintiff Eriksson engaged in constitutionally protected civic petitioning as an appointed Phase One lived-experience committee member on the State of Oregon Middle Housing and Oregon Homes Rulemaking Advisory Committee (MHOR RAC), a body on which Defendant City maintained its own representative, submitting published public comments challenging municipal code enforcement and utility shutoffs that manufacture residential displacement. In close temporal proximity, on June 22, 2026, Plaintiff Eriksson requested a reasonable 60-day accommodation schedule to complete yard maintenance and debris remediation. On June 23, 2026, Assistant City Manager and ADA Coordinator Alexandra Troutman issued a written denial on behalf of the City, stating: "the ADA does not excuse compliance with municipal code requirements in place to protect for the health and safety of the public" and declaring that "these obligations cannot be avoided and your request for ADA accommodation is denied."

31. On August 25, 2026, following Plaintiff Eriksson's August 20, 2026 emergency hospital evaluation for acute syncope resulting in a fall and facial trauma, Plaintiffs submitted a written Request for Disability Accommodation and Continuance at 1:13 p.m. for a 3:00 p.m. municipal hearing. At 1:29 p.m., the court clerk summarily denied the continuance without an interactive accommodation process. At 2:44 p.m., Plaintiff Eriksson requested a modified 20-minute accommodation window (to 3:20 or 3:30 p.m.) to manage post-collapse cognitive overload and review evidence. At 3:00:41 p.m., 41 seconds after the scheduled start time, municipal code enforcement personnel transmitted evidentiary photographs for review. The court proceeded in absentia at 3:13 p.m., heard ex parte code enforcement testimony, and entered a default judgment imposing $54,600.00 in cumulative penalties (stacking $300 daily fines across dual citations over 89 days) with a 10-day forced abatement mandate, which Plaintiffs are challenging on direct state appeal.

32. On September 8, 2026, the municipal court entered an Order denying relief from the judgment, whereupon Defendant Kennedy and municipal code enforcement personnel posted a 24-hour emergency door-hanger notice targeting curtilage debris and threatened immediate forced abatement.

## IV. CLAIMS FOR RELIEF

### COUNT I: Procedural Due Process
**(42 U.S.C. § 1983, Fourteenth Amendment)**  
*(By Both Plaintiffs Against Defendants City of Oregon City, Konkol, Fraijo, and Walker)*

33. Plaintiffs incorporate by reference the factual allegations in paragraphs 1 through 32.

34. The Fourteenth Amendment guarantees procedural due process before the government deprives a person of a protected property interest. Under Oregon law and Oregon City Municipal Code Chapter 13.04 (specifically OCMC §§ 13.04.040, 13.04.140, and 13.04.220), municipal water service is terminable only for cause, establishing a constitutionally protected property entitlement. *Memphis Light, Gas & Water Div. v. Craft*, 436 U.S. 1, 9-12 (1978); *Mathews v. Eldridge*, 424 U.S. 319 (1976).

35. Defendants deprived Plaintiffs of this protected entitlement without constitutionally required procedural safeguards:
    (a) Defendant City terminated and withheld municipal water service without providing written notice of dispute procedures, an opportunity to protest before an empowered employee, or a pre-deprivation hearing;
    (b) Defendant Konkol enforced administrative Policy 3-29 to maintain the shutoff, directed staff not to restore service without payment of unauthorized surcharges, and refused pre-deprivation administrative review (¶ 21);
    (c) Defendant Fraijo billed and demanded payment of unauthorized fees, refused Plaintiffs' cash and written tenders of delinquent balances, and refused to provide written dispute or hearing procedures (¶¶ 16-18); and
    (d) Defendant Walker authorized Work Order 24-000915 to remove the water meter and install a multi-ton concrete vault block over the connection, and under Work Order 25-001106 directed staff to refuse valve restoration (¶¶ 14-15, 29).

36. As a direct and proximate result of Defendants' actions, Plaintiffs suffered 510 days of continuous utility deprivation, severe medical emergencies, exacerbation of documented disabilities, and substantial economic and emotional harm.

### COUNT II: First Amendment Retaliation
**(42 U.S.C. § 1983)**  
*(By Both Plaintiffs Against Defendants Konkol, Kennedy, Fraijo, Walker, and Henderson)*

37. Plaintiffs incorporate by reference the factual allegations in paragraphs 1 through 32.

38. The First Amendment protects the rights to free speech, peaceful court petitioning, and participation in public rulemaking without retaliatory municipal punishment. *Capp v. County of San Diego*, 940 F.3d 1046, 1053 (9th Cir. 2019); *Coszalter v. City of Salem*, 320 F.3d 968, 973 (9th Cir. 2003).

39. Plaintiffs engaged in protected First Amendment activity by litigating quiet title (Case No. 24CV21417), asserting defenses resulting in a September 3, 2024 municipal trial acquittal, serving a March 24, 2025 Tort Claim Notice, petitioning for a state-court TRO, and submitting published public comments as an appointed member of the State MHOR Rulemaking Advisory Committee challenging municipal code enforcement displacement (¶¶ 11, 22, 26, 28, 30).

40. Defendants took adverse retaliatory actions that would chill a person of ordinary firmness from continuing to exercise First Amendment rights:
    (a) Defendant Kennedy hand-delivered backdated violation notices on July 22, 2024; posted a backdated red "UNSAFE" placard accompanied by five police officers on September 4, 2024, less than 24 hours after Plaintiff Eriksson's municipal court acquittal; and issued escalated demand letters threatening $300 daily penalties (¶¶ 19, 23, 26);
    (b) Defendant Fraijo suppressed a $2,863.77 third-party curing check for four months while disclosing confidential account records to adverse third parties (¶ 25);
    (c) Defendant Walker authorized concrete vault deployment and valve-opening refusals in direct retaliation for Plaintiffs' court filings (¶¶ 15, 29);
    (d) Defendant Konkol affirmed and maintained the utility shutoff following Plaintiffs' quiet title filings and administrative petitions (¶ 21); and
    (e) Defendant Henderson, on November 16, 2025, refused to permit utility crews to open the water supply valve under a Circuit Court TRO unless Plaintiffs executed an un-drafted release of liability, conditioning compliance with a judicial order on the surrender of constitutional petitioning rights (¶ 29).

41. Plaintiffs' protected petitioning and speech were substantial and motivating factors for Defendants' adverse actions, directly causing 510 days of water deprivation, unlawful placarding, and $54,600.00 in retaliatory fine demands.

### COUNT III: Substantive Due Process (State-Created Danger)
**(42 U.S.C. § 1983, Fourteenth Amendment)**  
*(By Both Plaintiffs Against Defendants City of Oregon City, Konkol, and Walker)*

42. Plaintiffs incorporate by reference the factual allegations in paragraphs 1 through 32.

43. The Due Process Clause of the Fourteenth Amendment prohibits municipal officials from affirmatively placing an individual in a position of known, severe danger to bodily health and personal safety in a manner that shocks the conscience. *Kennedy v. City of Ridgefield*, 439 F.3d 1055, 1061 (9th Cir. 2006); *Wood v. Ostrander*, 879 F.2d 583, 588 (9th Cir. 1989).

44. Defendants acted with deliberate indifference to Plaintiffs' lives and safety by executing affirmative acts that exposed Plaintiffs to severe, foreseeable physical peril:
    (a) Defendant Walker authorized the physical disconnection and concrete vault encasement of Plaintiffs' municipal water supply in July 2024 during a severe Pacific Northwest heatwave with temperatures reaching 100 degrees Fahrenheit, despite actual notice of Plaintiff Buckhout's heat-critical Graves' disease and Plaintiff Eriksson's medical fragility (¶¶ 11, 15-16);
    (b) Defendant Konkol repeatedly refused cash and written payment tenders, affirming the total physical deprivation of water for 510 consecutive days (¶¶ 17, 21); and
    (c) Defendants City and Konkol maintained the shutoff and red-placarding despite an official November 1, 2024 determination by Building Official James Sayers establishing that the prolonged shutoff was actively contaminating the dwelling's living quarters with hazardous sewer gases (¶ 24).

45. Defendants affirmatively created and maintained an unlivable, toxic domestic hazard, directly inflicting an acute Graves' disease crisis, severe asthma attacks, sepsis recovery disruption, and profound physical distress.

### COUNT IV: Fourth Amendment (Unreasonable Seizure of Property and Curtilage)
**(42 U.S.C. § 1983)**  
*(By Both Plaintiffs Against Defendants Kennedy, Walker, and City of Oregon City)*

46. Plaintiffs incorporate by reference the factual allegations in paragraphs 1 through 32.

47. The Fourth Amendment protects individuals against unreasonable seizures of property and unauthorized, non-consensual intrusions into residential curtilage without a judicial warrant or exigent circumstances. *Soldal v. Cook County*, 506 U.S. 56, 61-62 (1992); *Horne v. Department of Agriculture*, 576 U.S. 350, 358 (2015).

48. Defendants meaningfully interfered with Plaintiffs' possessory property interests and invaded their curtilage without legal authority:
    (a) Defendant Walker directed utility crews to enter Plaintiffs' curtilage, seize the water meter fixture, and deploy a multi-ton concrete vault block over the connection under Work Order 24-000915, physically dispossessing Plaintiffs of their utility connection (¶¶ 14-15);
    (b) Defendant Kennedy, accompanied by five police officers, entered Plaintiffs' curtilage on September 4, 2024 without a warrant, ordered occupants off the premises under threat of arrest, and posted a backdated red "UNSAFE" placard, dispossessing Plaintiffs of lawful residential occupancy (¶ 23); and
    (c) Defendant City executed and maintained these physical seizures and curtilage entries without obtaining an administrative inspection warrant or judicial order.

49. As a direct result, Plaintiffs suffered unlawful dispossession of their home, exclusion from their curtilage, panic, and severe trauma.

### COUNT V: Disability Discrimination
**(Title II of the Americans with Disabilities Act, 42 U.S.C. § 12132 & Section 504 of the Rehabilitation Act, 29 U.S.C. § 794)**  
*(By Both Plaintiffs Against Defendant City of Oregon City)*

50. Plaintiffs incorporate by reference the factual allegations in paragraphs 1 through 32.

51. Title II of the ADA and Section 504 of the Rehabilitation Act prohibit public entities receiving federal financial assistance from excluding qualified individuals with disabilities from participation in, or denying them the benefits of, municipal services, programs, or activities, and require reasonable modifications in policies, practices, or procedures. 42 U.S.C. § 12132; 29 U.S.C. § 794; 28 C.F.R. § 35.130(b)(7); *Duvall v. County of Kitsap*, 260 F.3d 1124, 1135-38 (9th Cir. 2001); *Mark H. v. Lemahieu*, 513 F.3d 922, 937 (9th Cir. 2008). On information and belief, Defendant City receives federal financial assistance within the meaning of § 504, including federal WIFIA loan funding and Community Development Block Grants (CDBG) (¶ 3).

52. Plaintiffs are qualified individuals with documented physical and mental impairments, including PTSD, MDD, acute sepsis recovery, heat-critical Graves' disease, asthma, and severe mobility limitations, that substantially limit major life activities (¶¶ 1-2).

53. Defendant City acted with deliberate indifference to Plaintiffs' statutory rights under Title II and Section 504:
    (a) Following Plaintiff Eriksson's August 21, 2024 written accommodation request detailing documented disabilities, Defendant City refused to engage in an interactive process, and City Manager Konkol affirmed that the City would make no modification to its billing or shutoff procedures (¶¶ 20-21);
    (b) In response to Plaintiffs' June 22, 2026 request for a 60-day accommodation schedule to remediate curtilage debris resulting from the prolonged shutoff, Assistant City Manager and ADA Coordinator Alexandra Troutman issued an outright categorical refusal on June 23, 2026, declaring in writing that *"the ADA does not excuse compliance with municipal code requirements in place to protect for the health and safety of the public"* and that *"these obligations cannot be avoided and your request for ADA accommodation is denied"* (¶ 30); and
    (c) On August 25, 2026, following Plaintiff Eriksson's emergency hospital evaluation for acute syncope and head/facial trauma, Defendant City's municipal court denied a requested 20-minute medical continuance without an interactive process, entering a default $54,600.00 penalty judgment in absentia (¶ 31).

54. None of Plaintiffs' requested accommodations sought debt forgiveness or a fundamental alteration of municipal operations. Defendant City's intentional and categorical refusals directly excluded Plaintiffs from essential municipal utility services and code compliance programs by reason of disability, inflicting severe medical crises, prolonged trauma, and catastrophic fine-stacking.

### COUNT VI: Fair Housing Act Retaliation & Interference
**(42 U.S.C. § 3617)**  
*(By Both Plaintiffs Against Defendant City of Oregon City)*

55. Plaintiffs incorporate by reference the factual allegations in paragraphs 1 through 32.

56. Section 3617 of the Fair Housing Act makes it unlawful to coerce, intimidate, threaten, or interfere with any person in the exercise or enjoyment of, or on account of having exercised or enjoyed, any right granted or protected by the Fair Housing Act. 42 U.S.C. § 3617; *Walker v. City of Lakewood*, 272 F.3d 1114, 1128 (9th Cir. 2001); *Brown v. City of Tucson*, 336 F.3d 1181, 1191-93 (9th Cir. 2003).

57. Defendant City deployed a coordinated apparatus of weaponized utility shutoffs, multi-ton concrete barriers, termination of franchised trash services, unannounced police curtilage intrusions, and excessive municipal fine-stacking to coerce, intimidate, and interfere with Plaintiffs' lawful residential occupancy and force them to vacate their dwelling.

58. Defendant City undertook these coercive measures on account of Plaintiffs' disabilities, requests for reasonable accommodations, and protected opposition to discriminatory municipal practices, directly inflicting constructive eviction, physical trauma, and loss of the peaceful enjoyment of their home.

### COUNT VII: Monell Municipal Liability
**(42 U.S.C. § 1983)**  
*(By Both Plaintiffs Against Defendant City of Oregon City)*

59. Plaintiffs incorporate by reference the factual allegations in paragraphs 1 through 32.

60. Under 42 U.S.C. § 1983, a municipality is liable for constitutional deprivations inflicted pursuant to an official policy, an established custom or practice, a decision by an authorized final policymaker, or a failure to train demonstrating deliberate indifference. *Monell v. Dep't of Social Servs.*, 436 U.S. 658, 690-94 (1978); *Pembaur v. City of Cincinnati*, 475 U.S. 469, 480-84 (1986); *City of Canton v. Harris*, 489 U.S. 378, 388 (1989).

61. Defendant City's policies, customs, and policymaker decisions were the moving force and proximate cause of Plaintiffs' constitutional injuries under four distinct grounds:
    (a) **Official Policy (Management Policy 3-29):** The City maintained an administrative cost-recovery policy that billing and public works personnel enforced to assess unauthorized $1,194.12 barrier surcharges and condition utility restoration on payment of disputed fees without an administrative dispute mechanism (¶¶ 15, 21);
    (b) **Final Policymaker Ratification:** Under the Oregon City Charter and OCMC Chapter 2.16, City Manager Tony Konkol is the Chief Executive and final administrative policymaker. City Manager Konkol personally affirmed and ratified the 510-day utility shutoff and the refusal of accommodations. Furthermore, Building Official James Sayers acted under municipal authority when determining that the shutoff caused sewer gas contamination; municipal leadership ratified this condition by maintaining the shutoff rather than abating the hazard. *Pembaur*, 475 U.S. at 483-84; *Larez v. City of Los Angeles*, 946 F.2d 630, 646 (9th Cir. 1991);
    (c) **Widespread Custom and Practice:** The City maintained an entrenched practice of weaponizing utility disconnections into code enforcement proceedings, cancelling franchised solid waste disposal, red-placarding residences without judicial warrants, and stacking excessive civil fines to force residential displacement of vulnerable occupants; and
    (d) **Failure to Train and Deliberate Indifference:** In response to formal public records requests seeking Title II ADA compliance policies, grievance procedures, and staff training records, Defendant City produced no responsive policies or training materials, demonstrating a complete failure to train municipal personnel regarding reasonable accommodations, as confirmed by ADA Coordinator Troutman's written declaration that the ADA does not apply to municipal code obligations (¶¶ 30, 32).

62. As a direct and proximate result of Defendant City's policies, customs, ratification, and failure to train, Plaintiffs suffered 510 days without running water, severe physical and psychological trauma, and the deprivation of their constitutional and statutory rights.
