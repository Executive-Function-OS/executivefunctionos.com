                                          DISCUSSION

       This Court concludes that Plaintiffs’ complaint should be dismissed for failing to state a

claim. Plaintiffs do not provide factual allegations to form any cognizable claim for relief,

though leave should be granted to amend the Complaint. Accordingly, this Court also denies

Plaintiffs’ request for a TRO or a PI since both are equitable remedies that require a “clear

   A. Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 8(a)(2)

       Plaintiffs’ claims do not meet the Rule 8(a)(2) threshold for stating a claim for relief.

Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 8(a)(2) requires a complaint to contain “a short and plain

statement of the claim showing that the pleader is entitled to relief.” Fed. R. Civ. P. 8(a)(2). This

requirement is necessary to “give the defendant fair notice of what the . . . claim is and the

grounds upon which it rests.” Conley v. Gibson, 355 U.S. 41, 47 (1957). This standard “does not

require ‘detailed factual allegations,’” but does demand “more than an unadorned, the-defendant-

unlawfully-harmed-me accusation.” Iqbal, 556 U.S. at 678 (quoting Twombly, 550 U.S. at 555).

“A pleading that offers ‘labels and conclusions’ or ‘a formulaic recitation of the elements of a

cause of action will not do.’” Id. (quoting Twombly, 550 U.S. at 555). Courts need not credit

legal conclusions that are couched as factual allegations. Iqbal, 556 U.S. at 678. Even under

notice pleading, courts are not meant to “piece together a theory of liability from a string of …

assertions.” Valadez-Lopez v. Chertoff, 656 F.3d 851, 859 (9th Cir. 2011) (cleaned up).

       Plaintiffs’ Complaint does not meet this standard. It is difficult to determine what

Defendants did that violated the rights guaranteed to Plaintiffs under the law. Although

Plaintiffs’ allegations appear to center on the City shutting off Plaintiffs’ water, Complaint, ECF

1 ¶ 1, the Complaint features numerous unrelated claims. As an example, the Complaint alleges

## III. TIMELINESS AND STATUTORY TOLLING

9. **Two‑Year Limitations Period & Multiple Independent Tolling Grounds**: Section 1983 claims in Oregon are governed by the two‑year personal injury statute of limitations in ORS 12.110(1). Plaintiffs' claims are fully timely and subject to the following independent tolling doctrines:

   **Continuing Violation Doctrine**: The City's 510-day water deprivation was not an isolated discrete act, but an unbroken, daily denial of an essential human necessity. The Ninth Circuit recognizes that when a defendant's conduct constitutes a "continuing violation," the plaintiff may recover for the entire period of the violation. *See Green v. Los Angeles County Superintendent of Schools*, 883 F.2d 1472, 1480 (9th Cir. 1989) (continuing violation exists where "a series of related acts, one or more of which falls within the limitations period" constitute one unlawful practice); *see also O'Loghlen v. County of Orange*, 229 F.3d 871, 873 (9th Cir. 2000) ("if a discriminatory act takes place within the limitations period and that act is 'related and similar to' acts that took place outside the limitations period, all the related acts, including the earlier acts, are actionable as part of a continuing violation"). Each day without water during the 510-day deprivation constituted a fresh injury, and the continuing nature of the deprivation supports tolling. *See also RK Ventures, Inc. v. City of Seattle*, 307 F.3d 1045, 1062 (9th Cir. 2002) (holding that time-barred discrete acts are not independently actionable but may serve as "background evidence" of discriminatory motive, while rejecting continuing violation doctrine for discrete acts under *National Railroad Passenger Corp. v. Morgan*, 536 U.S. 101 (2002)).

   **Fresh, Discrete 2026 Constitutional Violations**: Within the immediate 2‑year window (2024 to 2026), Defendants engaged in a series of fresh, discrete violations, including the June 23, 2026 categorical ADA denial by Defendant Troutman, the August 20, 2026 denial of accommodation following Plaintiff Eriksson's syncope collapse, the August 25, 2026 entry of a $54,600.00 default judgment in absentia, and the impending September 4, 2026 forced abatement deadline.

   **Discovery Rule**: Plaintiffs did not and could not discover Defendant Fraijo's secret suppression and return of the $2,863.77 Shellpoint curing check, or her backchannel disclosures to private broker Damien Pogue, until discovery in state litigation in early 2026. Under *Bibeau v. Pacific Northwest Research Foundation Inc.*, 188 F.3d 1105, 1108 to 09 (9th Cir. 1999), the statute of limitations does not begin to run until a plaintiff has knowledge of the "critical facts" of the injury, including the fact of injury and its cause.

   **Equitable Tolling & Fraudulent Concealment**: Defendants actively concealed the retaliatory purpose of their actions, backdated municipal violation notices, and misled Plaintiffs regarding utility billing balances. Plaintiffs' pro se status and severe medical emergencies (sepsis hospitalization in May 2024, Graves' disease crisis in July 2024, syncope collapse in August 2026) warrant equitable tolling under *Bibeau v. Pacific Northwest Research Foundation Inc.*, 188 F.3d 1105, 1110 (9th Cir. 1999).

## V. PRIOR PROCEEDINGS, PRECLUSION MITIGATION, AND QUALIFIED IMMUNITY

20. **Preclusion Mitigation**: Plaintiffs' federal civil rights claims are not barred by claim preclusion (res judicata) or issue preclusion (collateral estoppel) under Oregon law or 28 U.S.C. § 1738:

   a. **Distinct Nucleus of Operative Fact** (*Drews v. EBI Companies*, 310 Or. 134, 141 (1990)): The state action (Case No. 24CV21417) concerned title instruments and the validity of a 2019 foreclosure judgment. The federal action arises from a separate operational transaction: the City's 510-day physical water deprivation, physical concrete vault obstruction, backdated citations, post‑acquittal red placarding, suppression of third‑party curing funds, and categorical ADA denials.

   b. **New and Later-Occurring Facts**: The operative factual predicate occurred after the state case was filed, including the June 25, 2024 water shutoff, the July 8, 2024 concrete block placement, the September 4, 2024 red placarding, the June 23, 2026 ADA denial, and the August 25, 2026 $54,600.00 default judgment. Because Oregon follows a transactional approach to claim preclusion, only claims arising from the "same factual transaction" are barred. *See Drews v. EBI Companies*, 310 Or. 134, 140 (1990). The federal claims arise from an entirely separate factual transaction that occurred after the state case was filed.

   c. **Non-Identical Parties**: Individual Defendants Konkol, Kennedy, Fraijo, Troutman, Henderson, and Walker were never parties to the state quiet‑title litigation.

   d. **Active Court of Appeals Appeal Defeats Finality**: Plaintiff Eriksson timely appealed in Case No. A191649. A judgment pending appeal is not final for preclusion purposes. *See Eichman v. Fotomat Corp.*, 759 F.2d 1434, 1438 (9th Cir. 1985) (applying California preclusion law and holding that "a judgment is not final for purposes of res judicata during the pendency of and until the resolution of an appeal").

   e. **Interlocutory Stay Denial Not on Merits**: The August 27, 2026 stay denial was an interlocutory procedural order, not a merits adjudication on federal claims.

21. **Qualified Immunity Mitigation**: Plaintiffs allege violations of clearly established constitutional and statutory rights that were known to every reasonable official at the time of the violations:

   - *Memphis Light, Gas & Water Div. v. Craft*, 436 U.S. 1 (1978): right to pre-termination notice and hearing for essential municipal utility services;
   - *Horne v. Department of Agriculture*, 576 U.S. 350 (2015) and *Loretto v. Teleprompter*, 458 U.S. 419 (1982): physical takings doctrine for physical obstructions of property rights;
   - *Village of Willowbrook v. Olech*, 528 U.S. 562 (2000): class-of-one equal protection standard against irrational, animus‑driven enforcement;
   - *Duvall v. County of Kitsap*, 260 F.3d 1124 (9th Cir. 2001): ADA Title II deliberate indifference standard;
   - *Edelman v. Jordan*, 415 U.S. 651 (1974) and *Pulliam v. Allen*, 466 U.S. 522 (1984): immunity doctrines do not bar claims for prospective declaratory and injunctive relief against state and municipal officers in their official capacities;

## V. CLAIMS FOR RELIEF

*(42 U.S.C. § 1983)*  
*(Against Defendants City of Oregon City, Konkol, Fraijo, Walker, and Henderson)*

42. Plaintiffs incorporate by reference paragraphs 1 through 41.
43. **Constitutional Right Violated**: The Fourteenth Amendment guarantees procedural due process before a municipality deprives a person of a protected property interest. *Memphis Light, Gas & Water Div. v. Craft*, 436 U.S. 1 (1978); *Mathews v. Eldridge*, 424 U.S. 319 (1976).
44. **Defendants Responsible**: City of Oregon City; Tony Konkol; Ashley Fraijo; Vance Walker; Ramon Henderson.

### COUNT II: First Amendment Retaliation
*(42 U.S.C. § 1983)*  
*(Against Defendants Konkol, Kennedy, Fraijo, Walker, Henderson, and Wilson)*

48. Plaintiffs incorporate by reference paragraphs 1 through 41.
49. **Constitutional Right Violated**: The First Amendment prohibits government officials from retaliating against individuals for exercising their protected rights to petition the government, litigate property claims, and obtain court acquittals. *Coszalter v.
