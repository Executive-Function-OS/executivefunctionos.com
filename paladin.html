01 — Evidentiary Foundations & Procedural Governance

The Governing Rules Still Matter—But So Does the Procedure

Property and commercial-law disputes are not decided by slogans. They are decided through specific statutes, procedural rules, evidentiary foundations, burdens of proof, and binding precedent.

The relevant question is rarely whether a modern financial system is opaque in the abstract. The question is whether a party can prove the elements required in the case before the court, at the time and in the manner the law requires—and whether the opposing party has preserved a concrete, legally material objection.

When debt transfers move across secondary markets, the underlying property remains in a real jurisdiction. When a claim seeks to alter possession of a home, the law demands legible proof: authenticated instruments, competent witness testimony under evidence rules, valid authority at the time of action, and compliance with governing statutory frameworks.

PALADIN helps users organize the documents needed to examine those questions without promising that any particular theory will succeed.

“The absentee owner of natural resources is enabled to make them a source of free income, that is to say make them assets, by the power legally conferred on him to withhold them from use until his charge for their use is allowed him.”

• Thorstein Veblen, Absentee Ownership and Business Enterprise in Recent Times (1923)

“Modern property rules increasingly turn land into an asset class for monetization, capitalization, and exploitation—encompassing absenteeism, concentration, commodification, financialization, and even dispossession.”

• Prof. Jessica A. Shoemaker, Papering Over Place: When Land Becomes Asset Class, in Research Agenda for Property Law (Edward Elgar, 2024)

A useful legal-information platform disciplines inquiry: it identifies what documents exist, who created them, what governing rules require, and whether proposed assertions are grounded in admissible evidence rather than assumption.

Complexity Does Not Eliminate Accountability DOCUMENTATION INTEGRITY

Mortgage servicing and securitization can distribute responsibilities among originators, investors, trustees, servicers, document custodians, and counsel. That complexity can make it difficult for a homeowner or other self-represented litigant to identify what happened, which records control, and which entity can testify competently about a particular event.

“Property law rules that once balanced the rights of mortgagors and mortgagees now completely fail to furnish aggrieved homeowners with meaningful relief when faced with wrongs that stem from the complexities of the securitization of mortgage loans and the acts of intermediaries... once a loan is securitized, its expectancy was realized by the sale of the securitized loan leaving some successor or servicer to benefit from windfall fees and profits largely in excess of or established outside the original contract.”

• Prof. Christopher K. Odinet, Modernizing Mortgage Law, 100 N.C. L. Rev. 89 (2021)

Complexity alone is not a defense. But it can create documentation, chronology, authority, and evidentiary questions that must be analyzed carefully and tied to the governing legal standard. Furthermore, as documented by Adam J. Levitin and Tara Twomey in Mortgage Servicing (28 Yale J. on Reg. 1, 2011), servicer compensation models can generate documentation conflicts and servicing errors across successive transfers. PALADIN helps users sort the record so those questions can be reviewed responsibly.

Integrity Over Friction: Technology Must Serve Truth PROCEDURAL ACCOUNTABILITY

PALADIN is not a plea for horse-and-buggy paperwork, nor do we oppose modern technological conventions such as Remote Online Notarization (RON), digital filing systems, or the Uniform Real Property Electronic Recording Act (URPERA). Technological modernization is vital. But efficiency cannot be purchased by abandoning evidentiary integrity.

“Thousands of borrowers lost their homes when employees of mortgage servicers notarized affidavits supporting scores of foreclosures without the signers present.”

• National Notary Association, Forensic Review of the Foreclosure Crisis & Consent Judgments

The lesson of the $25 Billion National Mortgage Settlement is clear: when institutions use automation as an excuse to eliminate human review, the integrity of the record is compromised. PALADIN emphasizes verifiable standards, multi-factor credential analysis, and tamper-evident audit trails. Technology must be used to prove the truth, not to paper over the absence of personal knowledge.

No Case Is “Indefensible” by Slogan RECORD-BASED EVALUATION

Neither capitulation nor overconfidence is a sound method. Some foreclosure cases may have no viable merits defense; others may contain procedural, evidentiary, timing, servicing, notice, accounting, or enforceability issues that warrant closer review.

“Most lawyers believe foreclosures are not defensible. Rather than working to save a home, most lawyers acting in good faith advise clients to sell their homes... Even when given in good faith, this is poor advice. Many foreclosures can be defended... Inherent in lawyers’ refusal to try to defend foreclosure cases are deep-seated prejudices against homeowners who fall into foreclosure... The difference between losing and saving her home was a competent, determined foreclosure defense.”

• Kelli Dudley, An Excerpt of Iniquity: How Court Systems, Attorneys, and Legal Aid Organizations Cheated Homeowners in Foreclosure, 12 DePaul J. for Soc. Just. (2019)

A responsible system does not tell a user that their case is unwinnable, and it does not promise that technical defects will erase an obligation. It helps the user make an organized, source-linked record so they can understand the case, meet deadlines, identify questions for legal assistance, and present supported information clearly.

The Evidence Gate: From Slogan to Record STRATEGIC DISCIPLINE

A litigant should not treat the phrase “standing” as a magic word. Whether a plaintiff may pursue a claim depends on the procedural posture, the law governing the claim, the evidence offered, and the timing of relevant events.

For Oregon judicial foreclosure, the inquiry may include whether the plaintiff was entitled to enforce the note at the time it initiated the action. Under Oregon precedent, a party that possesses an instrument indorsed in blank may qualify as its holder under the Uniform Commercial Code, and a subsequent transfer does not automatically defeat the action (see Nationstar Mortgage LLC v. Peper, 278 Or App 594 (2016); Nationstar Mortgage LLC v. Clackamas Federal Credit Union, 285 Or App 204 (2017)).

Likewise, Oregon's landmark MERS decisions (Brandrup v. ReconTrust Co., 353 Or 668 (2013); Niday v. GMAC Mortgage, LLC, 353 Or 648 (2013)) arose in the specific statutory context of the Oregon Trust Deed Act (ORS 86.735) and nonjudicial foreclosure. They do not establish a universal rule that any unrecorded transfer or MERS reference invalidates a foreclosure action.

For a claim involving a lost instrument, the statutory requirements of ORS 73.0309 matter; the workflow should therefore capture the asserted possession history, the explanation for nonproduction, the documents offered to establish the claim, and any indemnity or evidentiary issues—not simply label every lost-note declaration invalid.

PALADIN's audit prompts users to distinguish unsupported shortcuts from record-first questions:

Unsupported Shortcut Record-First Question
“The loan was securitized, so the debt is invalid.” What governing law connects the alleged transaction to a legally material consequence in this case?
“MERS was involved, so the foreclosure is void.” Is this a judicial or nonjudicial proceeding, what statute governs, and what did the relevant instrument and recorded documents show?
“They must show me the original note.” What does the applicable law require, what evidence has been filed, and what specific deficiency is supported by the record?
“The servicer is a stranger to the loan.” What role is asserted, what records are relied on, who can authenticate them, and does the issue affect an element, remedy, or admissibility?
“A chain-of-title gap wins the case.” Is there a legally recognized defect, was it timely raised, is it material, and can it be established with admissible evidence?
