Operating Principles

Six rules for responsible record work

RECORD-FIRST DISCIPLINE

PALADIN is not a system for manufacturing defenses. It is a system for organizing, testing, and presenting questions that the actual record may support.

01
Start with the record
Begin with the instruments, recorded documents, notices, docket entries, correspondence, and filed evidence—not a theory found online. If a proposition is not supported by a document, testimony, rule, or authority, it is not yet ready to be asserted.
02
Identify the procedural path
Determine the court, case posture, deadlines, governing statutes, rules, and binding authority before drafting a theory. In Oregon foreclosure matters, for example, the distinction between judicial and nonjudicial foreclosure matters. Brandrup and Niday address Oregon Trust Deed Act issues involving MERS and nonjudicial foreclosure, while Nationstar Mortgage, LLC v. Peper addresses standing in a judicial foreclosure setting. A tool should help users identify that distinction—not erase it.
03
Tie every factual statement to a source
Each material factual statement should lead a reviewer to a document, page, date, and, where relevant, docket location. No source link, no reliable record trail.
04
Separate evidence from inference
A document may establish what it says. It may support an inference. It may also leave a gap. Those are different things. PALADIN keeps direct document content, user interpretation, disputes, and unresolved questions distinct.
05
Treat missing records as questions
A missing assignment, incomplete archive, or unclear possession history may warrant investigation. It is not automatic proof of fraud, invalidity, or entitlement to relief. The relevant question is whether the gap is legally material, timely raised, and supportable through admissible evidence.
06
File cleanly; seek counsel when possible
Meet deadlines. Check the current rules. Protect confidential information. Obtain qualified legal advice whenever it is available. PALADIN helps organize the work. It does not represent a party, sign a filing, or appear in court.
Scope & Limitations Notice: PALADIN is an educational tool and does not constitute legal advice. It does not evaluate legal sufficiency, determine whether a defense will succeed, or represent parties in litigation. Users must confirm all deadlines, procedural rules, and filings.
Evidence Gate

Record-specific analysis, not foreclosure folklore

A missing assignment, MERS involvement, securitization, or a generalized demand to “show the note” does not automatically create a defense. PALADIN helps users distinguish broad theories from record-specific questions tied to governing procedure, admissible evidence, and controlling authority.

Common shortcut Record-first question
“The loan was securitized, so the debt is invalid.” What governing law connects the transaction to a legally material consequence in this specific matter?
“MERS was involved, so the foreclosure is void.” Is the matter judicial or nonjudicial? Which statutes govern? What do the instrument and recorded documents show?
“They have to produce the original note.” What does the applicable law require, what evidence has been filed, and what specific deficiency appears in the record?
“The servicer is a stranger to the loan.” What role does the party claim, what records support that role, who can authenticate those records, and why does it matter procedurally?
“A chain-of-title gap wins the case.” Is there a legally recognized defect? Is it material, timely raised, and capable of proof through admissible evidence?

04 — Evidentiary Triage & Intake Forensics

The work before the argument

High-volume civil litigation often produces poorly indexed PDFs, incomplete service records, transfers among servicers, and documents that are difficult to compare across time. PALADIN helps turn that material into a reviewable working record.

The Real-World Evidentiary Crisis

What the Distressed Litigant Actually Receives: The Unmanifested Archive

Explore 5,612 Evidence Records §

High-volume civil litigation—especially foreclosure and administrative enforcement—generates unindexed PDFs, multiple servicer transfers, fragmented payment histories, and disputed lost-note affidavits. Self-represented litigants and busy clinics struggle to turn this volume into a coherent, court-ready record.

Symptom 01 The Unindexed Export Trap

Servicers and agencies produce hundreds of pages of unorganized records. Key payment histories, notices, and correspondence are buried across disconnected attachments.

Symptom 02 Evidentiary Gaps & Transfer Gaps

Declarations frequently rely on records from prior entities without personal knowledge, creating hearsay and foundation questions under Oregon Evidence Code 803(6).

Symptom 03 Procedural Rejection

Filings get bounced for simple rule violations—margins, line numbers, missing exhibit tabs, or unverified citations—before the substantive issues are ever reviewed.
